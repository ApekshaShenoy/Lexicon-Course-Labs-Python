Running an AI model locally means it works entirely on your own device, so your data never leaves your machine. This gives strong privacy, no usage fees, offline access, and full control—but you’re limited by your hardware, and setup can be more technical.

Tools like Ollama make this easier by letting you download and run large language models locally, similar to how Hugging Face pipelines already run smaller models on your system.

Compared to earlier approaches: classical ML models are small, task-specific, and easy to run, while modern LLMs are large, flexible, and work through natural language prompts instead of fixed inputs.

Choosing between them depends on the task:

* Use classical ML for simple, well-defined problems.
* Use Hugging Face models for specific AI tasks like translation or classification.
* Use local LLMs for open-ended tasks like chat, summarization, or coding—especially when privacy matters.

Overall, local AI has become much more accessible. Even laptops can now run reasonably powerful models, though there’s still a trade-off between model size, speed, and capability. Smaller models are faster and easier to run, while larger ones are more powerful but require much stronger hardware.
